Final paper usage list for presentation and dissertation

Context:
- the project is now firmly about VR for teaching computer architecture
- the older DBMS direction should not be treated as a central research thread anymore
- papers in red are excluded
- all remaining papers should still be used to some degree where sensible

Use the papers in the following order of importance.

1. Core domain papers
These should carry the main related-work section because they are the closest to the final project.

- Dascalu et al. (2017)
  Title: Experiential Learning VR System for Studying Computer Architecture
  Use for:
  - direct precedent for VR in computer architecture learning
  - motivation for immersive visualization of hidden hardware processes
  - comparison point for scope and interaction design

- Tan et al. (2024)
  Title: VR Serious Game for Learning the Computer Organisation and Architecture Subject
  Use for:
  - closest implementation to the final prototype
  - serious-game framing
  - support for engagement and staged learning design

- Tlili et al. (2016)
  Title: Improving Learning Computer Architecture Through an Educational Mobile Game
  Use for:
  - evidence that interactive/game-based learning is useful for architecture topics
  - non-VR comparison point
  - motivation for moving beyond static material

- Alnuaimi and Awad (2025)
  Title: VR Environment of Digital Design Laboratory: A Usability Study
  Use for:
  - nearby hardware/systems education context
  - usability-study precedent
  - support for the evaluation framing

2. Core VR-in-CS-education papers
These should support the argument that VR is a meaningful teaching medium for abstract CS concepts, not just for architecture.

- Pirker (2020)
  Title: The Potential of Virtual Reality for Computer Science Education
  Use for:
  - general motivation for VR in CS education
  - support for interactive visualization of abstract concepts

- Agbo et al. (2021)
  Title: Application of Virtual Reality in Computer Science Education: A Systematic Review
  Use for:
  - systematic-review grounding
  - broader state of the field
  - positioning the project within CS education literature

- Janani et al. (2026)
  Title: Virtual Reality Applications in Computer Science Education: Scoping Review
  Use for:
  - newest broad review
  - high-level field positioning
  - support for arguing that the area is active and still evolving

3. Core VR-education / design-constraint papers
These should support design decisions, limitations, and why the study matters.

- Cook (2019)
  Title: Challenges and Strategies for Educational Virtual Reality
  Use for:
  - practical constraints of educational VR
  - accessibility, deployment, and usability concerns
  - limitations / future work discussion

- Samala et al. (2025)
  Title: Virtual Reality in Education: Global Trends, Challenges and Advances
  Use for:
  - broad educational-VR context
  - high-level motivation and adoption framing

- Korkut and Surer (2022)
  Title: Visualization in Virtual Reality: A Systematic Review
  Use for:
  - visualization choices
  - clutter, readability, and interaction-design tradeoffs
  - justification for spatial guidance and simplified phase presentation

4. Transferable abstract-visualization papers
These should not dominate the story, but they are still useful because they deal with abstract structures and immersive visualization.

- Lee et al. (2020)
  Title: Data Visceralization: Enabling Deeper Understanding of Data Using Virtual Reality
  Use for:
  - the argument that VR can deepen intuitive understanding
  - caution around complexity and cognitive overload
  - support for the “guided, not overloaded” design philosophy

- Oberhauser (2018)
  Title: Database Model Visualization in Virtual Reality: A WebVR and Benediktine Space Approach
  Use for:
  - transferable precedent for abstract-structure visualization in VR
  - support for the claim that CS concepts beyond physical labs can benefit from immersive representation

- Oberhauser (2019)
  Title: Exploring WebVR and Native VR Concepts
  Use for:
  - background on implementation tradeoffs in VR delivery
  - minor support only, mostly if discussing platform choices or accessibility

- Kellmann et al. (2024)
  Title: Visualization and Exploration of Linked Data using Virtual Reality
  Use for:
  - transferable support for graph/structure visualization in VR
  - evidence that VR can help explore abstract relationships spatially

5. Weak-fit but still usable background papers
These are no longer part of the central story, but can still be cited lightly if needed.

- Bertino et al. (2005)
  Title: Integration of Virtual Reality and Database System Techniques
  Use for:
  - historical background only
  - shows that the link between VR systems and data-driven structure representation is not new
  - do not foreground this paper

- Kewat et al. (2024)
  Title: Enhancing Academic Learning through AR/VR: A 3D Visualization Approach for DBMS
  Use for:
  - lightweight proof-of-concept support
  - only as a transferable example of immersive visualization in CS education
  - do not let this paper steer the argument back toward DBMS

- Soflano, Connolly, and Hainey (2015)
  Title: Adaptive Games-Based Learning to Teach SQL
  Use for:
  - support for guided/adaptive learning design
  - only indirectly relevant now
  - useful when discussing learning-mode versus practice/test-mode scaffolding

6. Explicitly excluded
- Hadjiliasi et al. (2024)
- Bagci et al. (2025)
- Gomez et al. (2022)
