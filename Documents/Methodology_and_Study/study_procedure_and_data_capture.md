# Study Procedure and Data Capture

This file documents the study procedure as it is actually being run, rather than how a perfectly idealized study might look on paper.

That difference matters, because the dissertation should reflect the real workflow used with participants.

---

## 1. High-Level Study Structure

Each participant session follows the same broad pattern:

1. consent and background capture
2. short tailored refresher
3. optional short baseline probe using approved question-bank items
4. VR session using selected instructions and modes
5. post-session questionnaire and knowledge check
6. open-ended feedback and researcher notes

This is best described as a **structured guided session with post-interaction evaluation**.

---

## 2. Session Step-by-Step

### Step 1. Participant Information and Consent

Before the session begins, the participant:

- reads the Participant Information Leaflet
- has the opportunity to ask questions
- signs the consent form before taking part

This is the formal start of the research process.

### Step 2. Section A Background Questions

The participant then completes the background section of the Microsoft Form.

This captures:

- age confirmation
- prior VR use
- prior computer architecture exposure
- self-reported familiarity and confidence
- topics they have already studied
- topics they usually find difficult

This section is essential context for later interpretation.

### Step 3. Tailored Refresher

After background capture, the participant receives a short verbal refresher tailored to their prior experience.

This refresher is intentionally high level.
Its purpose is:

- to align terminology
- to reduce avoidable confusion
- to ensure the participant can meaningfully engage with the VR environment

It is **not** intended to replace what the participant will learn inside the prototype.

Typical refresher topics:

- what an assembly instruction looks like
- what the datapath represents
- what the major stages do
- how registers, memory, and immediate values differ
- where control choices matter

### Step 4. Baseline Question Subset

Before VR, the participant can be given a short subset of already approved question-bank items.

These baseline items focus on:

- program counter
- fetch
- instruction register
- stage order

This gives the study a small pre-intervention checkpoint without introducing a new questionnaire instrument.

### Step 5. VR Session

The participant then enters the VR environment.

The exact instruction subset is chosen according to:

- participant background
- available session time
- whether the participant appears comfortable enough to move into reduced-guidance modes

A typical path may include:

- 1-2 Learning mode instructions
- 1 Practice mode instruction
- optionally 1 Test mode instruction

During the session, the researcher intervenes only when needed.

This intervention is itself meaningful data, because it reflects how much support the participant required.

### Step 6. Post-Session Form

After removing the headset, the participant completes the remaining sections of the Microsoft Form.

These include:

- Likert ratings on system experience and perceived learning
- the knowledge check
- open-ended feedback

### Step 7. Researcher Notes

Alongside the formal form data, the researcher keeps a separate notes file for each participant session.

These notes record:

- which modes/instructions were attempted
- which concepts caused confusion
- how much prompting was required
- any strong verbal feedback given during or after the session

This is important because not every useful observation will naturally appear in the form response sheet.

---

## 3. What Is Being Captured

The session captures four types of evidence.

### 3.1 Background context

Who the participant is, in learning terms:

- prior experience
- prior confidence
- likely weak areas

### 3.2 Immediate interaction evidence

What happened during use:

- how they navigated
- where they hesitated
- how much support they needed
- whether they could progress from Learning to Practice or Test

### 3.3 Post-session perception

What they felt about the system:

- usability
- clarity
- engagement
- confidence
- whether it helped them understand

### 3.4 Post-session concept evidence

What they could answer after the session:

- selected architecture questions
- broader knowledge-check performance

---

## 4. Why the Procedure Is Defensible

The procedure is defensible because it matches the real educational position of the prototype.

The project is not trying to throw a participant into VR with zero preparation and claim that the system alone should teach everything from scratch.

Instead, the study reflects a more realistic supplemental-learning setting:

- the learner receives a short orientation
- the VR tool is then used to make abstract execution flow more tangible
- the participant is evaluated afterward on both experience and understanding

That is a much more credible educational use case.

---

## 5. Recommended Thesis Description

If a concise write-up is needed in the dissertation:

> Each participant completed a structured session consisting of informed consent, background screening, a short tailored refresher on relevant computer architecture concepts, a VR task sequence using selected Learning/Practice/Test instructions, and a post-session evaluation comprising Likert-scale ratings, a knowledge check, and open-ended feedback. Separate researcher notes were maintained to document observed difficulties, required support, and comparative remarks made during the session.
