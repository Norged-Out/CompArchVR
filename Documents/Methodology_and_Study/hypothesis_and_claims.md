# Hypothesis and Claims Draft

This file exists to separate three things clearly:

1. what the dissertation can reasonably hypothesize
2. what the study can likely support
3. what the project should **not** claim

That separation is important because the prototype is strong enough to support meaningful educational claims, but not broad enough to justify exaggerated ones.

---

## 1. Recommended High-Level Hypothesis

The safest main hypothesis is:

> A structured VR learning environment that externalizes instruction execution into visible, spatial, and interactive steps can support learners’ understanding of selected single-cycle MIPS datapath concepts and will be perceived as a useful supplement to traditional teaching materials.

This is a strong enough hypothesis to matter, but not so strong that it becomes indefensible.

---

## 2. Suggested Sub-Hypotheses

### H1. Usability and clarity

Participants will generally report that the system is understandable, navigable, and usable after a short introduction.

Why this matters:

- if the interface is too confusing, the educational value collapses
- usability is part of whether the intervention can function as a teaching tool at all

### H2. Conceptual support

Participants will report that the environment helps them understand instruction flow, datapath stages, and component roles more clearly than static materials alone.

Why this matters:

- this directly addresses the “abstract and invisible process” problem that motivated the project

### H3. Guided-to-independent progression

Participants will be more capable of completing tasks with reduced support after first using the guided mode.

Why this matters:

- the three-mode structure is one of the project’s core educational design choices
- if learners cannot progress, then the mode design loses much of its pedagogical meaning

### H4. Post-session knowledge evidence

Participant responses on the post-session knowledge check, together with the short baseline probe and observational notes, will suggest improvement in selected targeted concepts.

Why this matters:

- this gives the dissertation something more substantive than “they liked it”

### H5. Supplement rather than replacement

Participants will tend to position the system as a helpful complement to traditional explanation rather than as a full replacement for lectures, diagrams, or assignments.

Why this matters:

- this aligns with the actual project philosophy
- it is also much easier to defend than a replacement claim

---

## 3. Strongest Thesis Claims the Study Can Probably Support

If the data goes well, the dissertation should be able to support claims such as:

- the VR prototype was usable enough for participant testing
- participants generally understood the structure of the interaction after a short onboarding period
- the system helped make instruction execution more tangible and easier to follow
- the spatial and physical structure supported tracing and reasoning about datapath flow
- participants could move, to some degree, from guided completion toward more independent completion
- the prototype is promising as a supplemental computer architecture learning tool

These are realistic, meaningful, and publishable-style claims.

---

## 4. Claims That Should Be Avoided

The dissertation should avoid claiming:

- that VR is categorically better than traditional teaching
- that the prototype definitively improves learning outcomes in a causal experimental sense
- that the study demonstrates long-term retention
- that the prototype fully teaches computer architecture
- that the prototype replaces formal instruction
- that the results are broadly generalizable beyond the small exploratory participant set

These claims are too large for the current evidence base.

---

## 5. Recommended Main Dissertation Claim

If a single clean claim is needed, this is probably the best one:

> The prototype demonstrates that a carefully scoped VR environment can translate selected single-cycle MIPS instruction-execution concepts into a more tangible, spatial, and learner-driven experience, and early participant evaluation suggests that this approach is promising as a supplemental educational tool.

This is strong, honest, and appropriately bounded.

---

## 6. Why This Claim Structure Works

It works because it links:

- the educational problem
- the design response
- the implemented prototype
- the observed participant outcomes

without pretending that the project solved every architecture-teaching challenge.

That balance will matter a lot in the final writing stage.
