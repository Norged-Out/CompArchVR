# Comparative Analysis Strategy

This file exists because the phrase **“compared to traditional teaching methods”** is the hardest part of the dissertation question to defend cleanly.

The project does not currently operate as a formal lecture-vs-VR controlled experiment.
So the comparative angle has to be handled honestly and strategically.

---

## 1. The Core Problem

A direct experimental comparison would ideally require:

- one or more non-VR comparison groups
- standardized teaching exposure across groups
- equivalent assessment timing
- larger participant numbers
- stronger statistical power

The project does not have that structure.

So if the dissertation pretends that it does, the methodology becomes weak.

Instead, the comparative angle should be reframed as **limited, indirect, and exploratory**.

---

## 2. What Can Be Compared Right Now

Three comparisons are realistically available.

### Comparison A. Participant perception of VR relative to traditional materials

This is the cleanest and safest comparative layer.

The study already captures participant judgments about whether the VR system:

- improved focus compared to standard materials
- supported understanding better
- would be preferred as a supplement

This means the comparison exists through **participant perception**, not through a formal control condition.

### Comparison B. Pre-session baseline vs. post-session understanding

The approved question bank can support a small baseline subset before VR.

That gives a modest before/after comparison on selected concepts such as:

- program counter
- fetch
- instruction register
- stage order

This is not full pretest/posttest design, but it is still valid as a limited internal comparison.

### Comparison C. Guided vs. reduced-guidance performance inside the prototype

The three-mode structure itself creates a meaningful internal comparative progression:

- Learning mode: high support
- Practice mode: reduced support
- Test mode: minimal support

That allows the dissertation to ask whether participants could move toward more independent execution after guided exposure.

---

## 3. What Should Not Be Claimed

The dissertation should not claim:

- that VR was experimentally proven superior to lectures
- that the study directly compares two balanced teaching interventions
- that the results establish causal superiority of VR over traditional instruction

Those claims are too strong for the present design.

---

## 4. Best Defensive Framing

The cleanest wording is:

> The comparative element of the study is addressed indirectly through participant perceptions of VR relative to prior learning materials, a short baseline-to-post-session concept comparison using approved question-bank items, and observed progression from guided to less-guided performance within the prototype itself.

That sentence is extremely useful because it says exactly what the comparison is, without pretending it is more than it is.

---

## 5. Why This Is Still Acceptable

This strategy is still acceptable because the dissertation is fundamentally about evaluating a prototype in context.

The study does not have to answer:

> Is VR universally better than traditional teaching?

It only needs to answer something closer to:

> Does this prototype appear educationally useful, and do participants experience it as a valuable supplement to the kinds of materials they normally rely on?

That is a real and meaningful comparative question.

---

## 6. How to Use This in Writing

When discussing results, the dissertation can say things like:

- participants frequently described the system as clarifying concepts that had previously seemed abstract in diagrams or lectures
- self-reported responses suggested that the VR prototype was perceived as a useful supplement to conventional materials
- the short baseline/post-session pattern suggested improvement on selected target concepts
- the mode progression indicated that guided support could be gradually reduced for some participants

These are all comparative statements, but they remain defensible.

---

## 7. Bottom Line

The comparative analysis should be described as:

- **indirect**
- **bounded**
- **participant-informed**
- **exploratory**

That is the right level of ambition for the current study.
