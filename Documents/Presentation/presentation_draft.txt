Slide 2 - Why This Problem Matters
Title:
Computer architecture is difficult because key processes stay invisible

Bullets:
- Students must understand how registers, control, ALU, memory, and PC updates work together during instruction execution.
- Much of that reasoning is dynamic and abstract, yet it is usually taught through flat static diagrams.
- Traditional teaching tools can help students reach the right answer, but often do less to build an intuitive mental model of the flow itself.

Visual:
- a datapath figure or a cropped screenshot showing multiple stations in the VR space

What to say:
- mention that you were a Computer Organisation teaching assistant at the University of Arizona for four consecutive semesters
- note that students repeatedly struggled with the same hidden-process problems: navigating the datapath, understanding control signals, and following value flow through the processor
- explain that the most effective help was usually drawing over the datapath on a whiteboard or iPad and guiding students through it step by step
- emphasize that mental-model formation was the real problem, not just factual recall
- present this project as an attempt to recreate that guided tracing process in a self-paced spatial format

Slide 4 - Research Gap, Question, and Learning Outcomes
Title:
There is still room for a focused VR system for datapath reasoning

Bullets:
- Existing work shows promise, but focused architecture-specific VR learning tools remain limited.
- Many prior examples are proof-of-concept systems, broad surveys, or adjacent educational tools rather than a tightly scoped datapath teaching experience.
- This project asks whether VR can help learners reason through instruction execution more clearly and confidently.

Research question:
To what extent does an immersive virtual reality-based learning environment improve students' understanding of abstract computer architecture concepts compared to traditional teaching methods?

Learning outcomes:
- decode the relevant fields of supported single-cycle MIPS instructions
- trace instruction flow and data movement through the major datapath stages
- explain the role of the major datapath components involved in instruction execution
- select the correct operands and control-facing choices for supported instructions
- predict the resulting register, memory, and PC changes for the supported instruction set
- complete supported instruction walkthroughs with reduced or no guidance

Visual:
- minimal text slide

What to say:
- transition from literature to your contribution
- note that the project was shaped around building these learning outcomes progressively rather than treating architecture learning as one vague goal
- if needed, link this back to the teaching motivation: the outcomes formalize the exact skills students repeatedly needed help with during office hours
- stress that the prototype is not just "a VR demo," but a system built around explicit learning targets


Slide 5 - System Overview and Scope
Title:
The prototype teaches datapath flow through three progressively stricter modes

Bullets:
- Learning mode: guided walkthrough with structured support and explicit explanation
- Practice mode: reduced support, explicit instruction decoding, and limited hints and attempts
- Test mode: strict assessment version with no lesson or hint panels and minimal tolerance for mistakes
- Scope is intentionally limited to a single-cycle MIPS baseline rather than a full architecture simulator

Visual:
- screenshot of Intro UI or scene map
- optionally a tiny 3-box mode summary

What to say:
- explain that all three modes share one core world and lesson architecture
- stress that the project focuses on a single-cycle MIPS baseline
- mention that Learning, Practice, and Test loosely align with the progression from guided recognition and tracing to independent execution
- briefly note that this progression was intentional both pedagogically and for implementation discipline


Slide 6 - Core Interaction and Implementation Choices
Title:
VR is used only where physical or spatial interaction supports learning

Bullets:
- Physical instruction fetch and handoff
- Register and datapacket manipulation to trace operand flow
- World-space phase stations for IF, ID, EX, MEM, WB, and PC update
- UI used only for prompts, lookup support, feedback, and validation
- Instruction and info catalogs keep lesson content authored while phase controllers keep the runtime flow reusable

Visual:
- 2 to 4 screenshots: fetch, decode/register scan, ALU, settings/tutorial or map

What to say:
- this is a strong "design decisions" slide
- mention that not every action was made physical on purpose
- explain that realism was trimmed whenever it added friction without adding learning value

Slide 9 - Key Design Decisions
Title:
The project evolved toward guided spatial reasoning rather than full simulation

Bullets:
- Single-cycle datapath chosen over a larger architecture simulator
- Three-mode structure introduced to separate teaching, practice, and assessment
- Interaction was simplified where realism would have added friction without learning value
- Guidance, UI, and feedback were repeatedly refined for first-time usability
- Late-stage work shifted from feature expansion to build stability and participant readiness

Visual:
- optional before/after or phased-evolution summary

What to say:
- this is where you briefly acknowledge design pivots
- keep it short and reflective
- frame the pivots as deliberate scope control, not backtracking


Slide 10 - Limitations and Future Work
Title:
The prototype is complete for study use, but there is clear room to expand

Bullets:
- Current scope focuses on a single-cycle MIPS foundation
- Instruction coverage is intentionally selective rather than exhaustive
- Pipelining, hazards, multicycle behavior, and richer jump-family support remain future work
- Further studies could test learning impact more deeply across larger participant groups and over longer exposure

Visual:
- simple future-work list or roadmap

What to say:
- frame limitations as deliberate scope control rather than failure
- emphasize that a narrower but functioning educational tool is stronger than an unfinished all-encompassing simulator


Slide 11 - Conclusion
Title:
VR can support computer architecture learning by making datapath reasoning visible, paced, and interactive

Bullets:
- The prototype turns hidden execution flow into an embodied learning experience.
- It now supports Learning, Practice, and Test modes in a stable study-ready build.
- The participant study will help determine how much this improves understanding, usability, and perceived learning.
